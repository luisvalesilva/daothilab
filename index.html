About us

An international team of molecular virologists

We are an international team of scientists who enjoy studying viruses. We use stem cell-derived culture systems to study hepatitis E virus-host interactions.

Our lab is part of the Center of Integrative Infectious Disease Research (CIID) at the Heidelberg University Hospital. Our research is generously supported by the Chica and Heinz Schaller Foundation.

Science

Our work

Using stem cell-derived systems to unravel the hepatitis E virus life cycle.

With about 20 million infections each year, hepatitis E virus (HEV) represents a significant global health burden. Despite growing awareness, the comprehension of this virus and its life cycle at the molecular level remains scarce. A contributing factor is the necessity to grow the virus in cell culture systems usually based on aberrant cancer cells.

In order to overcome these limitations, we use embryonic or induced pluripotent stem cell (hESC/iPSC)-derived cell culture models, in particular stem-cell derived hepatocyte-like cells (HLCs).

HEV is a positive-strand RNA, non-enveloped virus (nHEV), which gains a membranous host-derived envelope when budding from cells — called quasi-enveloped HEV (eHEV). The projects in our lab are geared towards studying the role of the quasi-envelope during different HEV life cycle steps: entry, innate immune sensing, assembly, and secretion in and from polarized cells. We further explore novel treatment options.
